A 2×L40S node (96 GB) with FSDP can train up to **~3B dense params** (48 GB model states sharded across 2 GPUs + activations = ~40 GB each). But you need another dimension: a 3B model's model states are 48 GB. Sharded across 2 GPUs = 24 GB each. Plus ~16 GB activations per GPU = 40 GB per GPU. That fits on L40S (48 GB) but tightly. Batch size will be very small. ### With 2 nodes (4× L40S, 192 GB total, 4 GPUs): Sharding across 4 GPUs with FSDP: - 7B model: 112 GB model states ÷ 4 = 28 GB per GPU + ~20 GB activations = 48 GB → **barely fits** - That's the practical ceiling for this cluster configuration **But** this assumes perfect FSDP efficiency, minimal activation memory, and no overhead. The SMDM paper found: > MDMs (masked diffusion models) need **16× more training compute** to reach the same validation loss as an autoregressive model. So if training a 1B AR model to quality level Q costs: | Model | Compute | Time (4× L40S) | Quality | |-------|---------|-----------------|---------| | AR 1B | 1× | ~1 week | Q | | MDM 1B | 16× | **~16 weeks** | Q | | AR 125M | 0.016× | ~3 hours | Q−δ | | MDM 1B with 1× compute | 1× | ~1 week | Q−δ ≈ AR 125M | **A 1B MDM trained with the same compute as a 1B AR model achieves roughly the quality of a 125M AR model.** You're spending 1B-param compute for 125M-param quality. This is the fundamental economic challenge. ## The Autoregressive Baseline Comparison For context, here's what you could train on the same cluster with AR: | Model | Hardware | Time | Quality (PPL) | |-------|----------|------|---------------| | AR 1B | 1× L40S | ~3 days | Good | | AR 3B | 4× L40S | ~2 weeks | Very good | | AR 7B | 4× L40S | ~4-6 weeks | Excellent | The AR versions are **16× faster to train** for the same quality. This is the real reason diffusion LMs aren't replacing ARMs for training — and why the attention is on inference benefits. ## What About MoE? You mentioned DiffusionGemma is 26B MoE with 4B active. What if you trained an MoE diffusion LM? **Good news**: MoE doesn't add much training FLOPs per token (most params are dormant). **Bad news**: MoE adds memory. All 26B experts must be loaded in memory, even if only 4B are active. - 26B MoE model states (FP32 master): 26B × 16 bytes = **416 GB** - Even with 4× L40S (192 GB total): you'd need **FSDP + offloading to CPU RAM** Your L40S nodes have 64 GB RAM each × 2 nodes = 128 GB CPU RAM. Even CPU offloading can't save a 416 GB model. **MoE diffusion LMs need A100 80GB clusters or TPU pods.** ## The Bottom Line **Forget training a DiffusionGemma-class model.** The hardware gap is 2-3 orders of magnitude. **What IS realistic on 2×L40S nodes (4 GPUs):** | Model Size | Feasibility | Time | Use Case | |-----------|-------------|------|----------| | ≤300M | ✅ Practical | 2-3 days | Education, domain-specific LM | | 1B sub-optimal | ✅ Feasible | 1-2 weeks | Proof of concept | | 1B Chinchilla-optimal | ⚠️ Painful | 3-4 weeks | Research experiment | | 3B+ | ❌ Not worth | 6+ weeks | Quality too low for cost | **If you really want to train a diffusion LM that does something useful, the sweet spot is 300M-1B with domain-specific data (not general text).** That way the quality floor doesn't matter as much — you're optimizing for a narrow task where even "imperfect" generation is valuable. **Want to discuss what that would look like?** I can sketch a training recipe for, say, a 300M masked diffusion LM trained on Portuguese text — leveraging your language advantage rather than competing on raw scale.
