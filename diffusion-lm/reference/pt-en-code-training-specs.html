How We Speed Things Up

For your model sizes (300M to 3B), the main lever is data parallelism — each GPU holds a full copy of the model and processes different data. Gradients are synced via all-reduce once per step.

For larger models (3B+), FSDP helps by sharding model states across GPUs, trading memory for a small communication overhead. This lets you use larger batch sizes than pure data parallelism.

Strategy When to use Memory per GPU Comm overhead Works on your cluster?
Data Parallel (DP) Model fits in one GPU Full model copy Gradient all-reduce once/step ✅ Yes (model sizes ≤1B)
FSDP Model barely fits / need larger batch ~1/N of model states Gather/scatter per layer (overlapped with compute) ✅ Yes (all model sizes)
Tensor Parallel Very large models, need NVLink speed ~1/N of each layer All-reduce per operation (very frequent) ❌ No (needs NVLink)
Pipeline Parallel Extremely deep models (40+ layers) ~1/N of layers Activations transferred between stages ⚠️ Works but adds bubble overhead

Scenario A: 300M Model — The Sweet Spot

Model fits comfortably in one GPU (~15 GB). Pure data parallelism gives near-linear scaling.

Cluster GPUs Global batch/step Steps (6B tokens) Time per step Total time Speedup Cloud cost
1 node (baseline) 1× L40S 32 × 1024 = 32,768 183K 0.50s 25.4 hours 1× ~$20
2 nodes 4× L40S 32 × 4 × 1024 = 131,072 45.8K 0.50s + 0.15s comm = 0.65s 8.3 hours 🟢 3.1× ~$13
4 nodes 8× L40S 32 × 8 × 1024 = 262,144 22.9K 0.50s + 0.25s comm = 0.75s 4.8 hours 🟢 5.3× ~$15
8 nodes 16× L40S 32 × 16 × 1024 = 524,288 11.4K 0.50s + 0.40s comm = 0.90s 2.9 hours 🟢 8.8× ~$22

At 8+ GPUs, communication overhead starts eating into returns. Going from 4→8 GPUs gives 1.7× more speedup (not 2×). Beyond 8 GPUs, you'd need faster interconnects (e.g., InfiniBand) to keep scaling.

Bottom line for 300M: 2 nodes (4 GPUs) is the sweet spot — 8 hours instead of 25, for just $13. Going to 8 GPUs gets you under 3 hours but costs more and wastes compute on communication.

Scenario B: 1B Model — The Workhorse

Fits on one GPU (~26 GB) with room for modest batch sizes. Data parallel on multiple GPUs lets you increase throughput.

Cluster GPUs Global batch/step Steps (20B tokens) Time per step Total time Speedup Cloud cost
1 node (baseline) 1× L40S 16 × 1024 = 16,384 1.22M 2.0s 28.2 days 1× ~$300
1 node, 2 GPUs 2× L40S 16 × 2 × 1024 = 32,768 610K 2.0s + 0.1s (intra-node PCIe) = 2.1s 14.8 days 🟡 1.9× ~$320
2 nodes, 4 GPUs 4× L40S 16 × 4 × 1024 = 65,536 305K 2.0s + 0.30s = 2.3s 8.1 days 🟡 3.5× ~$320
4 nodes, 8 GPUs 8× L40S 16 × 8 × 1024 = 131,072 152.6K 2.0s + 0.50s = 2.5s 4.4 days 🟢 6.4× ~$360

With FSDP instead of pure DP on 8 GPUs, you can increase per-GPU batch from 16 to 32 (freed VRAM from model sharding), cutting steps in half:

Cluster (with FSDP) GPUs Global batch Steps Time Speedup from baseline
4 nodes, FSDP 8× L40S 32 × 8 × 1024 = 262,144 76.3K 2.5s 2.2 days 🟢
8 nodes, FSDP 16× L40S 32 × 16 × 1024 = 524,288 38.1K 3.0s 1.3 days 🟢
Key insight: FSDP on 8 GPUs (4 nodes) brings 1B training from 4 weeks → 2.2 days. That's a 12.8× speedup — and costs about the same as single-GPU training ($360 vs $300) because you pay for 8 GPUs but only for 2.2 days instead of 28.

The Real Bottleneck: Not Training Time

After running the numbers, here's what I'd highlight:

Training the 300M model on 2 nodes (4 GPUs) for 8 hours gives you a working diffusion LM. The question isn't "can I scale to more GPUs?" — it's "is the quality good enough for my use case?"

Before scaling to 1B/3B with a cluster, train the 300M model first. You'll learn:

  • Does the masked diffusion algorithm actually work well for PT+EN+Code?
  • Is 32K vocab enough? Do you need more code-specific tokens?
  • What does 75-100M AR-equivalent quality feel like? Is it useful?
  • How fast is inference? How many diffusion steps do you need for good quality?

The answers to these questions determine whether 1B/3B is worth scaling to — not the other way around.

In Case Your Cloud Provider Has Slower Networking

Interconnect Bandwidth Impact on 4-GPU training Impact on 8-GPU training
100 Gbps Ethernet ~12 GB/s 3.1× speedup (near-ideal) 5.3× speedup (good)
25 Gbps Ethernet (budget) ~3 GB/s 2.8× speedup (still good) 3.5× speedup (communication-heavy)
10 Gbps Ethernet ~1.2 GB/s 2.0× speedup (bottlenecked) 2.2× speedup (barely gains from 4→8)

Even at 25 Gbps (the most common budget cloud tier), you still get strong scaling up to 4 GPUs. The sweet spot shifts depending on your actual network.

These estimates assume 40% MFU on L40S, which is achievable with well-optimized PyTorch + FlashAttention. Real numbers may vary ±20%.
